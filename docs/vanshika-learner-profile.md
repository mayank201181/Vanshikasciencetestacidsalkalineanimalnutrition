### Patterns

1. **Neutralisation and salts: 3 marks, and the teacher's target.** She named the salt after the
   first-written reactant and used the wrong ending ("sulphur chloride"). She wrote "salt" where a
   chemical name was asked for. Our pack drilled salt naming heavily, but **every** stem put the
   hydroxide first, so a "take the first word" shortcut always worked in practice. It failed on
   the real paper, where the acid came first.
2. **Right idea, missing the keyword.** Teachers here mark on the term: "amino acids", "pH 7",
   "salt and water", "control". "It breaks down" and "they cancel out" scored 0.
3. **Working scientifically "explain why".** Controls and test duration lost 2 marks. She is
   excellent at the mechanical skills (graphs, trends, reading values, calculations: 8/8), but
   weaker at explaining *why* an experiment is designed the way it is.
4. **Recall in the reverse direction.** She knows what each nutrient does. She is weaker at
   going from nutrient → food source. Separation methods from Year 7 also turn up inside other
   topics.
5. **Self-diagnosis is too generous.** She ticked "silly mistake" for all of them. At least 4
   (salt naming ×2, the control, filtration) are gaps to teach, not slips.

### Strengths (keep, don't over-drill)

- Digestive system labelling and organ functions (6/6)
- pH scale in context, indicators in context, food tests (biuret, iodine), IV and predictions

## Build rules for her next pack

1. **Mirror the school paper's formats**, not just MCQ + free text: matching lines,
   fill-in-the-blank word equations, label-the-diagram, circle-the-answer, table completion,
   short "explain using the data" items. Keep a 45-mark, 45-minute mock with AN / AA / Skills
   style sections.
2. **Vary the surface form of every rule-based question.** Salts: acid first AND alkali first,
   "fill the blanks" and "name the product", both "sulphuric/sulphate" and "sulfuric/sulfate"
   spellings. Never let a positional shortcut work.
3. **Make the keyword the mark.** Every definition or "what happens" item must require the term
   ("amino acids", "pH 7", "salt + water"). Feedback should name the missing word explicitly.
4. **A working-scientifically block in every pack**: purpose of a control, why repeat, why
   run it longer, fair test, independent/dependent/control variables, judging a prediction
   against data. Use experiment scenarios like the school's (jelly + pineapple, James Lind).
5. **Ask in both directions**: job → nutrient AND nutrient → food source (best match when a food
   has several, e.g. leafy veg = minerals). Same for organs (job → organ, organ → job).
6. **Recycle earlier-year practical skills** inside the topic: filtration, evaporation,
   chromatography, condensation/distillation, heating safely.
7. **Exam-technique prompts in feedback**: "Name the product → the chemical name, not the
   type"; "Explain → quote the data"; "Litmus → state the colour you see".
8. **Make her classify each mistake honestly** after a mock: slip vs gap. Re-drill the gaps.
